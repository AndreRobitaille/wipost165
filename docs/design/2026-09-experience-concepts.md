# Experience concepts after the illustrated mockups

September 7, 2026. Exploration, not a selected design or implementation brief.

## The correction

The owner rejected the two illustrated mockups. Changing a hero illustration,
type treatment, and panel styling retained the same conventional website structure.
The car and grill had no established connection to the Post. Do not treat either
mockup as an approved direction or infer real Post activities from its sample data.

The request is for an original experience that makes prospective visitors feel
something: recognition, fellowship, anticipation, pride, and confidence about
coming along. The audience includes veterans with different civilian lives and
partners considering participation together. Women veterans belong in the veteran
audience in their own right. Existing members' fraternal identity remains central.

## References actually inspected

- [Take the Con](https://takethecon.org/): inspected the live home and About pages
  in a browser. The binder, side tabs, mission clock, orbital navigation, captions,
  and form-like information create a coherent setting. The theme organizes useful
  information throughout the site. The owner supplied this as their own site.
- [Console portfolio video](https://x.com/Angaisb_/status/2095964361105789424?s=20):
  direct X access failed, but the public post's video was retrieved through Twitter's
  syndication response. Reviewed ten frames spanning the approximately twenty-second
  clip. The console screen contains the portfolio itself: menu, biography, work,
  toolkit, and contact. Buttons, selection states, and transitions express the maker's
  playful identity. This was video review, not hands-on testing of that portfolio.

Their useful lesson is coherence between subject, presentation, and interaction.
Neither requires copying a binder, console, or physical object. The Post's design
should remain distinctive when someone opens an event or plans a visit.

## Concept 1: In good company

**Emotional proposition:** I can picture being part of this group.

The organizing form is a shared table, interpreted through graphic design rather
than a rendering of an imaginary clubhouse. An oversized rounded table shape crosses
the viewport; its near edge is open to the visitor. A few consenting members' first
names and brief introductions sit around the perimeter. Different service eras and
civilian lives can be represented without arranging people by rank or seniority.

The central surface is the working interface. Its clearly labeled views are People,
What's happening, and Your first visit. Choosing a person brings their short account
into the same space; choosing an event brings in its practical details. A modest
transition maintains the sense of gathering around the same place. Dates and a direct
contact action remain available without exploring people first.

The artwork would come from composition, the oversized shape, typography, names,
and movement. The official Legion mark keeps its own clear space. No decorative
cars, grills, drinks, military props, or invented building are needed.

On a phone, the near edge of the table frames one readable view, with ordinary
labeled controls. No miniature overhead room, dragging, compulsory rotation, or
tiny labels. All views have normal URLs, and essential tasks also have direct links.

The first-visit view answers the social uncertainty as well as logistics: what the
occasion is like, whether arriving alone is normal, how introductions work, and
whether guests or children are welcome at that particular occasion. Those answers
must reflect real Post practice. A named welcome contact is useful only if someone
has actually agreed to that role.

**Distinctive risk:** The table could become a clever container for ordinary content.
It earns its place only if the people-to-occasion-to-first-visit relationship is
apparent and useful. A screenshot of a table with slogans would repeat the failure.

**Small content requirement:** A few short, approved member introductions. Portraits
and optional voice recordings could help but are not required for a concept test.
Production must not invent testimony, hosts, or attendance.

## Concept 2: Same oath. Different lives.

**Emotional proposition:** There are people here whose lives overlap with mine.

The organizing form is a set of individual paths that come together. The opening
composition uses broad, precisely drawn blue and red bands with differing origins,
spacing, and direction. Each carries an actual person's story across service,
civilian life, and their connection to the Post. It resembles a contemporary graphic
installation rather than a patriotic background image.

Selecting a path opens a brief personal account. Moving toward the point where
paths meet reveals a shared occasion or contribution. The same paths connect People,
Gatherings, and Service views: an event reveals the people involved; a person's
story leads to a relevant public occasion when one exists. This is deliberately
curated editorial content, not a public member directory or inferred social graph.

The visual behavior expresses the proposition: different lives intersect here.
It should remain understandable in a still frame and with reduced motion. On a
phone the paths run down the page, with large labeled stops and normal links.
No forced animation, personality quiz, branch selection, or disclosure of personal
circumstances is required to browse.

Military service establishes the common bond, while the stories include civilian
life. Avoid making recent separation, combat experience, age, marital status, or
continued military self-identification an assumed entry requirement.

**Distinctive risk:** An attractive abstract diagram may still feel impersonal.
The real accounts are essential, not content to add after the design is finished.
It also needs care to avoid visually dividing men into veterans and women into
partners. People are introduced in the terms they choose.

**Small content requirement:** Several genuine accounts, gathered through a few
short conversations. A useful prompt is what made the first visit easier and what
kept the person coming back. Do not require an archival project or staged photoshoot.

## Concept 3: Home ground

**Emotional proposition:** What I do here matters to the place I live.

The organizing form is an illustrated, geographically grounded civic map of Two
Rivers. Its rivers, shoreline, streets, and public places create a visual identity
that does not require picturesque venue photographs. This would be a deliberately
composed town portrait with functional locations, not a stock map widget behind
a headline.

A simple date control changes the public occasions shown on the map. Opening one
reveals its purpose, people where approved, directions, and participation details.
Other views show verified examples of the Post's contribution to community life.
The visual emphasis moves between the place, the people, and the occasion within
the same map. No tactical symbols, targeting effects, invented activity, or private
member locations belong here.

On a phone, readable event details share space with a small location illustration;
the ordinary event list remains directly accessible. Geographic browsing is optional.
An out-of-town visitor can get directions without learning the map interaction.

**Distinctive risk:** This may communicate a civic institution more strongly than
veteran fellowship. A retired veteran could admire its work while still having no
idea whom they would spend time with. It also depends on enough verified public
activity to make the map meaningful. Sparse activity must not be inflated.

**Small content requirement:** Confirmed public locations and a few current or
accurately dated past activities. No continuous stream of new photos is necessary.

## Audience roleplay

These are fictional visitor scenarios used to challenge the concepts. They are
not testimonials, interviews, demographic findings, or evidence of conversion.
Circumstances describe these individuals, not everyone of a given age or gender.

### A retired veteran, 64, coming alone

Inner question: "I could use something to look forward to. Am I going to walk into
a room where everyone already knows everyone?"

- In good company: "I can see who I might talk to. But where do I go when I arrive?"
  Strongest initial fit. Revise the visit details to explain an actual introduction,
  not merely give an address or say everyone is welcome.
- Same oath: "Someone else was out for years before joining. That helps." Strong
  recognition if a real account supports it. Avoid presenting only lifetime members.
- Home ground: "They do good things. Is this mostly asking me to volunteer?" Pride
  alone does not resolve the social question.

### A divorced veteran, 47, with an established civilian life

Inner question: "I'd enjoy knowing a few more people. I don't need a website telling
me I'm lost or trying to send me back to basic training."

- In good company: "This seems relaxed. Do I have to join before I can meet anyone?"
  Explain the actual route to visiting without forcing an application first.
- Same oath: "These people have whole lives beyond their service. I relate to that."
  Strongest fit, provided the copy does not diagnose loneliness or promise healing.
- Home ground: "Potentially worthwhile, but I already have a job." Keep fellowship
  legible; do not make a service commitment the price of social belonging.

### A partner, 39, considering an outing with a veteran spouse and children

Inner question: "Would we feel comfortable coming together, and what would we
actually be doing?"

- In good company: "I can imagine us there, but does this specific event welcome
  children?" Clear event-level information matters more than a family slogan.
- Same oath: "An account from someone with a busy household would make this feel
  possible. Don't make me read several stories to find the date."
- Home ground: "This makes it easy to see an actual thing we could do together."
  Strong practical fit if the event truly accommodates that household.

The useful action is sharing a clear invitation with a partner, not persuading one
adult to manage another adult's social life. Guest attendance and membership
eligibility must be explained separately.

### A woman veteran, 42, who is also a parent

Inner question: "Will they recognize me as the veteran, or assume I'm here with one?"

- In good company: "Introduce me on equal terms. Don't send me to a spouses area."
- Same oath: "This works if the actual people represented include lives like mine."
  Do not invent a woman member to manufacture that evidence.
- Home ground: "Useful information, but the map cannot answer the welcome question."

All concepts need language and participation routes that recognize women veterans
directly. A family's presence must not determine which membership information
someone is offered.

### A longtime member, 76

Inner question: "Does this still look and sound like our Post?"

- In good company: "Getting together and looking out for each other is familiar."
  Strong continuity without requiring the physical meeting room to be attractive.
- Same oath: "Keep our service and traditions visible; don't turn us into a generic
  social group." Connect fellowship to actual service and mutual helpfulness.
- Home ground: "I'm glad people can see what we contribute. Just let me find the
  next event quickly." Useful secondary direction with immediate direct navigation.

## Recommendation and a useful next proof

Explore In good company first and Same oath. Different lives. as the alternative.
They address belonging through different visual and interaction systems. Home ground
is worth retaining for community-service content but is less convincing as the
primary answer to the audience's social question.

Keep the next comparison narrow: the opening experience, one person/story, and the
transition into one event and first-visit details. Evaluate phone-sized still frames
and a short interaction recording together. A still can test visual identity; the
recording can establish whether the idea actually shapes navigation. Do not generate
another large illustration as a substitute for this proof. The concepts above remain
open hypotheses; no production implementation or public publication is authorized
by recording them here.

Across concepts, preserve direct access to events, contact, veteran help, membership
information, and the separate members site. Keep official branding intact, readable
type, keyboard operation, normal URLs and back behavior, and reduced-motion support.
The public calendar and approved stories can later use the proposed companion API;
none of this authorizes publishing private member records. Quiet or unavailable
calendar states must remain honest and useful.
