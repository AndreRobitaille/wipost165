# Community: Two Rivers, Wisconsin

This document describes the community American Legion Post 165 serves.
Read this before making decisions about website content, navigation,
communications, or public-facing features.

For the primary veteran audience, use the owner's October 8 distinctions in
[Veteran audiences in Two Rivers](AUDIENCE.md). Established local relationships,
chosen roots and recent arrival can lead to different reasons for seeking a
group. The broader observations below are context, not findings about every
veteran or a requirement to lead with community service.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Community Identity

Two Rivers is a small Lake Michigan city of roughly 11,000 people with a
strong blue-collar identity rooted in manufacturing, shipping, and local
industry. Many residents have deep family ties to the area, and it is
common for multiple generations of a family to have lived here. The city
has an older-than-average population and values continuity, civic
institutions, and local traditions.

People generally know one another, either directly or through family,
churches, schools, workplaces, or community organizations. Reputation
matters. Trust is earned slowly, and word of mouth remains one of the
community's most influential forms of communication.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Community Values

Residents generally value:

- Service to the community.
- Patriotism expressed through action rather than symbolism alone.
- Honoring military veterans and first responders.
- Local traditions and annual community events.
- Personal responsibility and volunteerism.
- Practical problem solving over ideology.
- Organizations that consistently show up and contribute.
- People who quietly do the work without seeking recognition.

Many residents support local organizations because they believe they are
important parts of the community, even if they are not personally active
participants.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Disposition

Residents tend to:

- Appreciate authenticity over polished marketing.
- Prefer positive, constructive messaging over public criticism, even
  when criticism is factually accurate.
- Be skeptical of organizations that appear self-promotional.
- Respond well to visible community involvement.
- Respect volunteer service.
- Prefer straightforward communication.
- Place greater value on visible action than prolonged discussion.
- Judge organizations by what they consistently accomplish rather than
  what they promise.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## How They Use This Site

Most visitors are occasional users.

They come because they want to:

- Learn what the Legion is and what it does.
- Find an upcoming event.
- Confirm the time or location of an activity.
- Learn about veterans services or community programs.
- Contact the post.
- Decide whether to attend an event, volunteer, donate, or become
  involved.

They rarely browse for long periods or explore every page. Most visits
begin with a specific question and end once they have their answer.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What They Need

- Immediate understanding of what the post is and why it exists.
- Easy access to upcoming events.
- Clear contact information.
- Confidence that information is current.
- Plain language instead of military or organizational jargon.
- Evidence that the post is active and engaged in the community.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## What They Do NOT Want

- Military acronyms without explanation.
- Internal Legion politics or organizational disputes.
- Long explanations of Legion structure or governance.
- Outdated event information.
- Empty pages or placeholder content.
- Corporate marketing language.
- Excessive emphasis on fundraising instead of service.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Editorial Voice

The website should reflect the character of the community.

### Tone

- Friendly without being casual.
- Respectful without being formal.
- Patriotic without being performative.
- Community-focused rather than organization-focused.
- Confident without boasting.
- Practical and action-oriented.

Lead with what fellowship could add to a veteran's life: company, shared
experience, being known and mutual helpfulness. Community service is a meaningful
opportunity for those who want it. Enjoying the company of other veterans is
also a sufficient reason to belong; avoid implying that a person must volunteer
or take responsibility for the organization to be welcome.

### Local Communication Style

People generally respond better to examples than arguments.

Show the Legion serving veterans, sponsoring youth, participating in
community events, presenting scholarships, conducting memorial
ceremonies, or supporting local causes rather than telling visitors the
organization is important.

When discussing challenges, emphasize what is being done to address
them. Residents tend to admire organizations that quietly roll up their
sleeves and solve problems more than organizations that spend time
explaining why the problems exist.

Avoid language that feels confrontational or accusatory. Even when
raising legitimate concerns, focus on constructive action and positive
outcomes whenever possible.

### Community Context

The American Legion is one of many long-standing civic institutions in
Two Rivers.

The website should present the post as part of the fabric of the
community—not separate from it. Veterans, families, schools, churches,
local businesses, nonprofits, civic organizations, and residents all
intersect through the work of the post.

Whenever possible, show the community benefiting from the Legion rather
than simply describing what the Legion does.
