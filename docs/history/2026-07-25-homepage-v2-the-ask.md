# Invitation experiment: The Ask

Historical summary, distilled on 2026-09-07. Superseded as a specification;
all prior design and implementation choices may be reconsidered.

The membership facts table was replaced by an invitation to contribute. The
reasoning was that busy veterans might respond to useful work before eligibility,
dues, or an abstract appeal to belonging. It was editorial judgment informed by
post conversations, not a measured conversion study or a claim about every veteran.

A named, reachable person and concrete examples of service made the invitation
more human. Dues, eligibility, and counts moved out of the homepage in that
iteration. Their future placement is open. Volunteer frequency and time
commitments were not safe to invent merely to make the invitation persuasive.

The pass also corrected unreadable date colors and experimented with modestly
sized photographs. A later iteration moved up to four photos into the top strap
and hid them when the strap was too narrow; missing photos left no placeholders.
Descriptions supplied text alternatives, with existing image descriptions as a
fallback. That solution addressed the available low-resolution images, not a
permanent rule about image sizes, counts, or mobile visibility.

The last source/mobile order was events, invitation, annual rhythm, then pillars.
Desktop grouped related date information in one column. Grid track sizing caused
an unwanted gap and heavy rules distracted from content. These examples support
checking real narrow and wide layouts and logical reading order; no old grid,
breakpoint, CSS value, or section order is required in Rails.
