# Homepage density experiment: The Board

Historical summary, distilled on 2026-09-07. Superseded as a specification;
all prior design and implementation choices may be reconsidered.

The prior homepage audit recorded 3,085 pixels of height, about 3.7 viewports,
and 426 words, with no date, photograph, person's name, or number other than 1919.
Six similarly shaped bands gave everything equal emphasis. Those measurements
describe a past design, not a target height for the next one.

The response was a compact answer board for two questions: when an activity
happens, and what the post is/how to get involved. It combined dated events,
meeting information, an annual rhythm, and a membership panel; the panel later
became an invitation to volunteer. A compact strap replaced the large hero.

The annual rhythm helped an empty calendar feel intentional, but most proposed
milestones were provisional. The quiet-season assumption also needs confirmation.
A future design should not turn those mockup details into factual events.

Useful observations were faster access to answers, differentiated visual
hierarchy, meaningful mobile reading order, clear audience labels, and useful
empty states. The board, year strip, column count, source section order, and
exact density are all open to replacement.
