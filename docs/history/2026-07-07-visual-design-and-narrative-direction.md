# Visual and narrative experiment

Historical summary, distilled on 2026-09-07. Superseded as a specification;
all prior design and implementation choices may be reconsidered.

The first pass looked like a wireframe: repeated centered bands, a generic hero,
unloaded font declarations, and inconsistent use of design tokens. The experiment
introduced “Still here. Still serving.” as a narrative of past trust, present
service, and an invitation to help shape the future.

Named local history was considered stronger evidence than broad nostalgic
claims. Robert E. Burns's biography remained missing; the design idea was not
proof of his story or the post's charter year.

The visual experiment used navy, cream, gold, and red; Fraunces for display and
Public Sans for body/UI; and a ceremonial motif with a prominent and a quiet
setting. Interior pages used the quieter expression to keep content readable.
These are historical choices, not a palette, font, or slogan requirement.

The rationale was to show actual work and invite participation rather than
promise future importance. Local photography could support that story when
available. Emblem use required attention to its official meaning and permitted
use. Current design and content choices remain open.
